# Shipwreck Explorer

**Pilot a submersible around a shipwreck, in your browser.** The first wreck is RMS Titanic, as it lies today 3,800 m down in the North Atlantic.

The site is modelled at full scale from published measurements and the 1911 builder's plans: the bow section, the stern section about 690 m to the south, the boilers and the debris field between them. Nothing is photographed or scanned. Every shape is generated by code from numbers, and every number is traced to its source in [`docs/SOURCES.md`](docs/SOURCES.md).

## Try it

**[experiments.optional.org.uk/shipwrecks/titanic](https://experiments.optional.org.uk/shipwrecks/titanic/)**

Open it in **Chrome** or **Edge** on a desktop computer, wait a few seconds while the site is built, then press **Dive**. It needs a browser with **WebGPU**.

You can also download [`index.html`](index.html) and open it directly: nothing to install, and it works offline.

The readout in the top left shows your depth, heading, height above the seabed and where you are on the wreck. When you are near something notable, a note at the bottom says what it is and which sources it rests on. The mini map in the bottom right shows the deck plan of the section you are beside, with its openings, or the whole site when you are away from the hull.

## Going inside

The bow section is hollow. Its plating, decks and bulkheads are solid, and every real opening lets you through:

- **The grand staircase shaft**, from the boat deck down to E deck. From it you can swim out along each deck.
- **The two funnel uptakes**, oval shafts leading down to the boiler rooms.
- **Hatches Nos. 1 to 3**, down into the holds.
- **The torn end**, where the boilers of boiler room No. 2 sit in the open, and a tear in the port side under the first funnel.

- **The port gangway door on D deck**, which has fallen away, straight into the first class reception room.

What you will find inside is what dives have reported as still there: the lift trunk with its iron grilles, the reception room and a corner of the dining saloon, the crew passage called Scotland Road on E deck, the Turkish bath cooling room on F deck, brass bedsteads where cabin walls have rotted away, and the boilers in their rooms. On the boat deck are the Marconi set and the captain's bath.

At the stern, the two main engines stand in the open at the torn end and you can swim between their columns. Aft of them the stern cannot be entered: its decks collapsed flat on to one another.

## What lives there

Rattail fish cruise just off the bottom. Brittle stars, sea stars and sea cucumbers sit on the mud. Squat lobsters, anemones, sponges and two kinds of coral have settled on the wreck itself. These are the animals recorded at the site, not decoration.

## How real is it

No sunlight reaches this depth, so by default the only light is your own lamps, and the water swallows it within a few tens of metres. That is what a dive on the wreck is like. The **survey light** (`V`) is not real: it lights the whole site evenly so you can see how the pieces lie.

What is measured and what is estimated:

- **Measured, with sources:** the ship's principal dimensions and deck heights, the hull's bulkheads and openings, the position of the stern relative to the bow, the orientation of the stern, where the hull parted and how it is bent, how deep the prow is buried, what survives on the decks and what has collapsed.
- **Estimated:** the exact hull curves (no table of offsets is in the public domain, so the form is fitted to the published dimensions and plans), the compass heading of each section (no source gives it in degrees), the positions of individual portholes, the detailed shape of the wrecked stern, and almost everything inside the hull apart from its decks, bulkheads, shafts and boilers.

`docs/SOURCES.md` lists every approximation under "Known approximations". If you know a public domain source that would let one of them be replaced with a measurement, please open an issue.

## Sources and licensing

This project uses only public domain reference material, plus facts (which cannot be copyrighted) cited from published sources.

- [`docs/SOURCES.md`](docs/SOURCES.md) is the log: every source, its licence status, a link to where it came from, and where in the code it is used.
- `reference/` holds local copies of the public domain plans and photographs. It is not committed. `docs/SOURCES.md` gives the link to download each file again.
- Copyrighted material is deliberately not used. That includes the 2022 Magellan scan, RMS Titanic, Inc. imagery, National Geographic photographs and modern redrawn plans. Material that is free but not public domain (CC BY, CC BY-SA) is not used either.

## Build it yourself

The readable source is in [`source`](source). You need [Node.js](https://nodejs.org) 18 or newer:

```bash
cd source
npm install
npm run build
```

This writes a new `index.html` next to this README. `npm run build:readable` writes an unminified version, which is easier to debug.

The live copy is published by a maintainer after changes are merged; it does not update on its own.

| File | What's inside |
|---|---|
| [`source/src/ship.js`](source/src/ship.js) | RMS Titanic as built: dimensions, hull form, decks and fittings, and the damage to each section |
| [`source/src/site.js`](source/src/site.js) | The wreck site: where each piece lies, and the points of interest |
| [`source/src/world.js`](source/src/world.js) | Places everything on the seabed: hull sections, boilers, debris, stones, rusticles |
| [`source/src/shaders-scene.js`](source/src/shaders-scene.js) | The look: seabed, lamp light, rust and paint, marine snow (WGSL shader code) |
| [`source/src/main.js`](source/src/main.js) | The renderer, the camera, the controls and the readouts |
| [`source/src/audio.js`](source/src/audio.js) | The soundscape |
| [`source/src/math.js`](source/src/math.js), [`shaders.js`](source/src/shaders.js), [`quality.js`](source/src/quality.js) | Noise, mesh helpers, shared shaders and detail levels |

To add another wreck, the work is a new pair of files like `ship.js` and `site.js`, and new entries in the sources log.

## Contributing

Contributions are welcome, from people working by hand or with an AI assistant. Corrections that replace an estimate with a sourced measurement are especially valuable: the open ones are listed under "Known approximations" in [`docs/SOURCES.md`](docs/SOURCES.md).

### The workflow

1. **Fork and branch.** Build with `cd source && npm install && npm run build:readable` and open `index.html` in Chrome or Edge.
2. **Make the change in `source/`.** The map of the code is in the table above.
3. **Source it.** Anything that sets a dimension, a position, or a statement about the wreck needs a source:
   - add the source to `docs/SOURCES.md` with its link and licence status, and add a row to "Used where";
   - put its id in a comment beside the number in the code, for example `[A1]`;
   - if it is a guess, mark it `est.` in the code and add it to "Known approximations".
4. **Use public domain material only.** Facts can be cited from any published source in your own words. Images, drawings, text and scan data can only be saved or reused if they are public domain or CC0. CC BY and CC BY-SA are not. Reference files go in `reference/`, which is not committed; log each one in `docs/SOURCES.md`.
5. **Record the decision.** If your change involves a choice (a new feature, a change of direction, picking one option over another), add an entry to [`docs/DECISIONS.md`](docs/DECISIONS.md): what was decided, why, and what else was considered, in plain words. Give it the next id.
6. **Rebuild.** Finish with `npm run build` so the committed `index.html` matches the source, and check it still loads.
7. **Commit and open a pull request.** Put a line `Decision: D008` (your entry's id) at the end of each commit message that carries the decision out. In the pull request, say what you changed and which sources it rests on.

### If you use an AI assistant

[`AGENTS.md`](AGENTS.md) holds these rules in the form an assistant reads. Codex, Cursor, GitHub Copilot's coding agent and most other assistants read that file on their own; Claude Code and Gemini CLI are pointed to it by `CLAUDE.md` and `GEMINI.md`. If yours does not pick it up, tell it to read `AGENTS.md` first. Two things to know:

- **Your prompts stay private.** After a session, `python3 scripts/export-prompts.py` saves the prompts you typed to `docs/prompts.jsonl` and `docs/PROMPTS.md` on your machine. It reads Claude Code's and Codex's session histories directly; with any other assistant, the assistant adds them itself with `--add`. Both files are gitignored and are never committed or shared.
- **The reasoning is published.** Your assistant writes the `docs/DECISIONS.md` entry from those prompts, in its own words, without quoting you. You are named only as you choose to be.

## How it was built

This project is built by directing AI coding assistants (so far, Claude Code). [`docs/DECISIONS.md`](docs/DECISIONS.md) records the choices that shaped it and the reasoning behind each, and commits name the decision they carry out, so `git log --grep "Decision: D003"` finds the work for any entry.
